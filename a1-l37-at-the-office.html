At the Office

Work & Workplaces
⏱ 60 min 📚 10 vocab words 🎯 Work & Workplaces
Section 1
Vocabulary
desk
a table where you work, usually with a computer
"I sit at my desk from nine to six."
meeting room
a room used for group discussions or presentations
"The meeting room is on the second floor."
colleague
a person who works with you
"My colleagues are very helpful and friendly."
printer
a machine that produces copies of documents on paper
"The printer is near the reception area."
deadline
the date or time by which something must be finished
"The deadline for the report is Friday."
presentation
when you show and explain information to a group
"I have a presentation to the board next Monday."
report
a written document with information or results
"I need to finish the monthly report today."
break
a short rest during the working day
"We usually have a break at eleven o'clock."
reception
the front area of an office building for visitors
"Please wait at reception — someone will come."
canteen
a place in a workplace where you can eat and drink
"We usually have lunch in the canteen."

Section 2
Grammar Focus

Location in the office: The printer is next to the door. The meeting room is on the second floor. Routine: I usually start at nine. We have lunch at two. Describing tasks: I am working on a report. I need to finish this by Friday.

Section 3
Reading

Pedro works in a large company in Madrid. His office is on the fifth floor of a modern building in the city centre.

He starts work at nine o'clock. First, he checks his emails and makes a list of tasks for the day. He works at his desk until lunchtime. The canteen is on the ground floor and he usually has lunch with his colleagues at two o'clock.

In the afternoon, Pedro often has meetings. The company has four meeting rooms on the third floor. He uses his laptop to give presentations.

At the end of the day, he checks his list and writes a short report for his manager. He usually leaves the office at six o'clock.

Pedro likes working in an office because he enjoys being with his colleagues. But sometimes he works from home on Fridays.

Did you know?
Spain has the longest working day in Europe. The average Spaniard works until 8 or 9pm — but also has the longest lunch break. However, Spain's productivity per hour is lower than countries with shorter days, leading many companies to change their working hours.
